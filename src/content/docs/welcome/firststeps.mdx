TangleGuard is the place for you see all visualizations and where you can configure and document your architecture.

When opening the TangleGuard Designer for a specific codebase for the first time, it will create a [configuration file](/start/configuration) for you.
within the repository.

<Steps>

1. **Explore your codebase with the Designer**
   View your codebase as a graph structure within the TangleGuard Designer.
   You can view the components of your codebase and how they are related to each other.

1. **Define Layers within the Designer**
   If you use a layered, onion-based or a hexagonal architecture in your codebase already, the you can define those layers within the Designer Desktop App. That way, TangleGuard can inform you about architecture drift. You can enforce those layers also with the TangleGuard CLI.
   Those layers are stored in the [configuration file](/start/configuration/) as mentioned above.

1. **Validate via Designer or CLI**
   Then, Validate your Architecture regularly. TangleGuard traverses your source code and compares the actual dependencies against the allowed dependencies.
   Once a violation is found, you better fix it. Alternatively, you enforce the rules via your CI/CD pipeline and eliminate not-allowed dependencies from the beginning.

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip">
  Use the Designer to explore your architecture and configure your target
  architecture.
</Aside>
